We took on our first fitout nine years ago as a two-person design team subcontracting the build to someone else. The gap between what we drew and what got built was frustrating enough, often enough, that we brought construction in-house within the first eighteen months. Everything since has been built on that decision.

## What hasn't changed

The fundamentals of a good fitout are the same as they were nine years ago: a design that respects how the space will actually be used, a build team that protects that design intent through construction, and a handover date that was realistic on day one rather than aspirational.

## What has

Timelines have compressed. A retail or F&B fitout that reasonably took four to five months in 2016 is now expected in eight to ten weeks, driven by rent-paying pressure and operators who've watched competitors open faster. That's only possible with parallel-tracked approvals and a build team that isn't waiting on design to finish before starting procurement.

Briefs have also gotten more specific earlier. Clients now arrive with brand guidelines, fixture specs, and sometimes a full design pack from an international franchise — our job has shifted from originating every design decision to translating and coordinating an increasing number of them against real UAE building constraints.

## The number that matters most to us

81 completed projects isn't the milestone we talk about most internally — repeat clients are. A meaningful share of our project list is a second, third, or fourth fitout for the same operator, which is the only client feedback that actually matters on a turnkey promise.
