A fitout doesn't fail in week one. It fails in week three — when the Civil Defence submission comes back marked up, the ceiling void turns out to be 80mm short of the drawing, and your opening date starts sliding a week at a time in a polite Thursday email.

Dubai asks more of an interior than good taste. It has to pass DEWA, Civil Defence, and Dubai Municipality, survive a landlord's fitout manual, and then hold up under real trading. The contractor you pick decides all three.

## How to choose between them

Four questions decide it, and they're worth asking in the first meeting rather than the third:

- **Have they built your sector, at your size, in the last two years?** Ask for three. Then go and stand in them mid-service — not on opening day. Check the grout by the entrance and the joinery nearest the till.
- **Who carries the approvals risk?** When a submission comes back marked up, whose programme absorbs it? A vague answer means it's yours. ([Here's how those timelines actually stack up](/journal/dubai-fitout-approvals-timeline).)
- **Is the MEP in-house or bought in?** Every handoff is a seam, and weeks disappear into seams.
- **What happens after handover?** The gap between a good fitout and a cheap one shows up around month four — usually to your customers first. Get the response time in writing.
