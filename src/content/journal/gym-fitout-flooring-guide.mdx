Across the gym fitouts we've delivered, flooring is the single spec decision operators most often revisit within the first year — usually because it was chosen on look and price rather than load and acoustic performance.

## Density matters more than thickness

A thicker mat isn't automatically a better one. Rubber flooring is specified by density as much as thickness, and a dense 15mm tile will outperform a low-density 20mm tile under repeated dropped-weight impact — which is the actual failure mode in a free-weight zone, not general foot traffic.

## Acoustic isolation is a structural question, not just a material one

In a multi-tenant building — which describes most retail and mall gym units in the UAE — dropped-weight noise transmits through the slab to the unit below regardless of how good the surface mat is, unless there's a proper acoustic underlay isolating the floor system from the structure. This has to be specified before the slab is poured or before the raised floor goes in, not retrofitted after a noise complaint.

## Zone the spec, don't blanket it

The cardio zone, the free-weight platform, and the studio floor all have different real requirements. Specifying one flooring product across the whole gym is usually either overpriced for the cardio zone or underspecified for the drop zone. Splitting the spec by zone is standard on every gym fitout we run.
