Every fitout in Dubai touches at least three approval bodies, and they don't run to the same clock. A project plan that assumes they do is the most common reason a "12-week fitout" becomes a 20-week one.

## The usual sequence

**Landlord or mall design approval** comes first and gates everything else — no authority will review a submission that hasn't been signed off by the landlord's design team. Malls in particular can take multiple review cycles if the concept drawings don't match their fitout manual on the first pass.

**Civil Defence** reviews fire and life safety separately from the general building permit, and typically wants final MEP drawings, not concept layouts — which means it can't start until the services design is genuinely locked.

**DEWA** approval for electrical and water connections runs on its own timeline again, often the longest single wait on the critical path for anything with a new kitchen line or increased power load.

**Dubai Municipality** (or the relevant free zone authority — DIFC and JAFZA run their own systems) issues the actual building permit and final completion certificate.

## What actually shortens the timeline

Running these in parallel rather than in sequence is the single biggest lever. That only works if MEP design is finished early enough to submit to Civil Defence and DEWA before the interior finishes are even fully specified — which is a scheduling discipline, not a shortcut.

The other lever is knowing, before submission, which of the four bodies is going to be the bottleneck for your specific site and scope, and front-loading that one.
