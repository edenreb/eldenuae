Bikanervala is a sweet house before it is a restaurant, and the brief was to keep that distinction legible. This is not fine dining and was never meant to read as it — it is a signature branch, where the room itself carries the weight that white linen would carry somewhere else.

The detail is drawn from Indian architectural vocabulary rather than applied over it as ornament. Arched niches set into the marble echo the jharokha, the projecting balcony window of Rajasthani havelis, each one holding a mirror in a brass surround. Above them a gilded rosette set with blue stone reads as a sun motif. Ribbed arcs of concealed light span the ceiling and land on the wall like structural ribs, so the ornament and the lighting are the same gesture.

The marble was cut specifically for Bikanervala — a bespoke tile rather than a stock format, so the surfaces belong to this brand and to no other room on the strip.

Fitout scope covered full MEP, structural ceiling support, and the joinery and lighting package for the dining hall.
